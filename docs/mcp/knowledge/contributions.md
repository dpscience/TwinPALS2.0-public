# Pile-up and contribution classes

First measure A and B independently from their ADC samples. Then match recorded analog contributions to each selected pulse's time support. A contributor qualifies when its onset is at or before the support end and its stored or reconstructed end reaches the support start. The reference path conservatively retains an earlier contributor when an end is unavailable. Attribution is temporal. Two visible peaks, two photoelectrons or mixed scintillation and Cherenkov light alone do not establish pile-up.

Each categorized pair gets one main class. Missing channel attribution gives unresolved. If either selected channel has more than one overlapping contribution, the union of their recorded decay IDs determines same-decay or multiple-decay pile-up. Otherwise, different decay IDs give accidental background. Two different energy roles in one decay give the correlated, ideal/intended topology. This label describes the simulated origin; detector timing and measurement broadening still apply.

For same-role pairs, record identity and primary-photon evidence distinguish the same gamma contributing in both detectors from two different 511 keV photons from one Na-22 annihilation. A backscatter claim needs recorded interaction and direction evidence. Read the selected transport path for its angle and detector. Co-60 uses 1173 and 1332 keV cascade roles and has no annihilation-pair class.

Subtypes retain physical channel order regardless of spectrum direction. For pile-up, count contributions by energy role in A and in B. For example, A with 511 + 1275 keV and B with 511 keV belongs to same-decay pile-up when all IDs agree. Changing one ID to another decay moves the pair into multiple-decay pile-up. A 511 keV contribution from one decay in each channel with different IDs is accidental when each channel has only one contributor. A later pulse starting after the selected support does not enter its pile-up count.

Subtypes partition their parent. For 1,000 pairs, a class of 200 with exclusive subtypes of 120 and 80 contributes 20%, split into 12% and 8% of the same population. Light origin and scattering describe additional properties of those pairs.
